We are all human.

We are inherently flawed.

Suffering is unavoidable.

Our brains want to protect us, but they do a poor job of it.

Defenses blind us. Avoidance keeps us stuck.

Fear of what could happen restricts what can be.

We get in our own way. A self-fulfilling prophecy.

We want to feel better, be better, do better. But the brain does not un-learn very well.

So we struggle. We strive. We worry. We feel bad. We feel down. We blame others. We withdraw.

We escape.

## The only way out

The way out of this cycle is not through shame, guilt, hardness, or discipline.

The only way out is through self-kindness.

Speak to yourself as you would speak to a friend you love very deeply.

We are SUPPOSED to love ourselves.

That isn't selfish, narcissistic, or inconsiderate. It is required of good humans to be kind to themselves. How can you show others true kindness if you don't know it for yourself?

If shame and guilt worked, we wouldn't need to keep experiencing those negative emotions. Kindness feels different. Self-compassion changes your expectations of others, and of yourself.

The answer to how we find happiness, in my professional opinion, is in being kind to yourself.

## It takes practice

I can teach you how to do this. It isn't easy. At times we'll be working against your biology, your sociology, and your physiology.

Change is slow. It happens gradually, over time. Bursts of insight may come, and they help. But self-kindness has to be practiced daily, alongside a consistent meditation practice, exercise practice, hygiene practice, and sexual practice.

Being self-kind is a vital part of happiness.

## The list of musts

### Breathing

Breathing is step one. Without the breath, we are more likely to get lost.

### Relaxing, physically

Yoga Nidra. Progressive muscle relaxation. Meditation. Anything that relieves anxiety and tension.

You must know relaxation very well to be able to use it in difficult times. That takes repeated effort. If you feel like you're failing, just keep trying. Don't let the judgment in. It will increase the tension.

### Listening

To everything, from silence to deep conversation.

Practice listening for as long as you can without interrupting. Even encourage the speaker to say more, while you continue to only listen.

### Smile

A smile can spark a small moment of joy. Sometimes the shift in your emotion is so quick and so slight you may not notice it at all.

The research agrees, with honest limits: across 138 studies, smiling tended to make people feel a little happier. The effect is small (Coles et al., 2019). Small still counts.

### Sleep

Consistency is the most important part. Routines before sleep, used consistently, can help your brain produce the right sleep chemicals at the right times.

### Hygiene and organization

Stay mildly to moderately aware of how clean your space is. That lets you take small, easy steps to keep it clean without:

1. Feeling bad that it got that way.
2. Feeling bad for not doing all of it right now.
3. Getting angry about what others did or didn't do.

And it gives you the freedom to let the cleaning go when something else needs your attention.

### Relationships

Getting into them. Finding friends. Accepting judgment. Being cautious of your own judgments.

Learn from others. And learn from your own awareness of your impact on others.

### Joy

Find it and savor it like the last bite, of the last piece, of the world's best dessert.

## Try this today

Catch one thing you say to yourself today. Then say it again, the way you'd say it to a friend you love very deeply.

Breathe. Be kind. Savor the last bite.
