A Florida State University student once interviewed me about remote learning and mental health. Classrooms have mostly reopened since then, but online classes, remote work, and long days alone at a screen are here to stay. So are the lessons.

Here are my answers.

## Have you seen the effects of remote learning on students' mental health?

Yes, and much of it has been negative.

Students with attention difficulties had a harder time staying on task, finishing tasks, finding motivation, and remembering information. Students with social anxiety showed a clear increase in symptoms and a stronger urge to avoid people. For students with depression, low motivation usually showed up as poor academic performance.

## What advice do you have for students who are struggling?

Change it up.

Don't sit at the same desk in the same apartment day after day. Spend an afternoon at your favorite coffee shop. Explore libraries all over town and campus. Study with other people, even if you're each doing your own work, so you still get real social contact.

If you're stuck in one space indoors, take frequent breaks outside and stretch often.

## How might remote learning affect behavior back in the classroom?

The more we practice a behavior, the better we get at it. If students aren't practicing sitting in a classroom, it's reasonable to expect some difficulty adjusting when they return.

## How can professors make remote learning easier?

Hybrid models, with some in-person and some remote learning, are best when possible.

Professors can encourage social connection, and even give credit for it. And they can understand that students differ in how well they learn from a screen. Some adjust extremely well. Others are suffering, and struggling much more than they will ever admit.

## Should schools keep using video conferencing like Zoom?

Yes. Video conferencing can be extremely useful in schools.

Now that it's widely accepted, students can learn from people far beyond their own campus: guest speakers, guest lecturers, and international or out-of-state students sharing what they know. It also gives teachers and professors more freedom to travel, for education or for fun, while still meeting their teaching goals.

## Try this this week

If you study or work from home, pick one day to work somewhere else, around other people. Notice your focus and your mood at the end of the day.

Change your space. Change your energy. Change your day.
