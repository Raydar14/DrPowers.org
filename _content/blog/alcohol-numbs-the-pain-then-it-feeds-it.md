"A drink helps my pain."

It's not wrong. In the short term, alcohol really does dull pain. The trouble is what daily drinking does over months: to your gut, to your nerves, and to the pain itself.

This isn't a lecture. Shame never helped anyone drink less. This is what's happening inside your body, so you can make your own call.

## Your gut is a border wall

Your intestines do three big jobs:

- Absorb nutrients from your food
- Keep bacteria and toxins out of your bloodstream
- Keep your immune system balanced

The lining of your gut is one cell thick. Those cells are held together by tight junctions, like mortar between bricks. When the mortar holds, nutrients get in and the bad stuff stays out.

## What daily alcohol does to the wall

**It loosens the mortar.** Alcohol and its breakdown products can damage gut cells and disrupt tight junctions. The lining gets leaky, and bacterial toxins (endotoxin) can slip into the bloodstream, where they drive inflammation and can contribute to liver injury (Bishehsari et al., 2017).

How strong is this evidence? Much of the step-by-step mechanism comes from animal and cell studies. In people, the picture is clearer for heavy drinking. In one study of people with alcohol dependence, a subset had increased gut permeability, and those people also had altered gut bacteria and higher depression, anxiety, and craving scores, which were still higher after three weeks without alcohol (Leclercq et al., 2014). That's a link, not proof of cause, and it doesn't tell us much about one or two drinks a day.

**It shifts your gut bacteria.** Chronic drinking is linked to bacterial overgrowth and an imbalance between helpful and harmful bacteria (dysbiosis) (Bishehsari et al., 2017). Human data are still limited, and most of the detailed findings come from rodents (Engen et al., 2015).

**It keeps the gut irritated.** Leaky walls plus unbalanced bacteria plus toxins in the blood add up to ongoing, low-grade inflammation, in the gut and beyond it (Bishehsari et al., 2017).

## Where IBS comes in

Irritable bowel syndrome affects about 5 to 10% of people at any given time. It's a problem with how the gut and the brain talk to each other: changes in how the gut moves, a gut that's extra sensitive to normal sensations (visceral hypersensitivity), and changes in how the brain processes those signals (Ford et al., 2020).

Some people with IBS have a leakier gut lining than people without it (Camilleri & Gorman, 2007). Researchers also suspect gut bacteria, immune changes, and low-grade inflammation play a role, but those findings are less consistent (Ford et al., 2020).

Here's what we can say about alcohol and IBS specifically. In a month-long diary study of 166 women with IBS, binge drinking (four or more drinks in a day) was most strongly linked to next-day diarrhea, nausea, stomach pain, and indigestion. Light and moderate drinking had little or no link to next-day symptoms. Women with diarrhea-type IBS were hit hardest (Reding et al., 2013).

So the connection is real, and it seems to depend on how much you drink at once. Whether daily alcohol's effects on the gut lining and bacteria actually cause or worsen IBS over time hasn't been directly tested. It's a reasonable theory. It isn't settled science.

## Alcohol and pain: short-term relief, long-term trap

### Why it helps tonight

Alcohol is a central nervous system depressant. A review of 18 lab studies found that at about the legal driving limit (roughly three to four drinks), alcohol raised people's pain threshold a little and cut pain intensity by about 1.25 points on a 0 to 10 scale (Thompson et al., 2017).

That's real relief. It's also why it's so easy to reach for.

### Why it doesn't last

Alcohol doesn't fix the injury, the inflammation, or the reason you hurt. It changes how loudly you hear the alarm. The fire's still burning.

And the relief fades fast. Your body builds tolerance to alcohol's pain-dulling effect, so it takes more to get the same relief. When you stop, withdrawal can make you more sensitive to pain (hyperalgesia), which makes it harder to stop. People with alcohol use disorder have more chronic pain than the general population, and people with chronic pain often drink to manage it (De Aquino et al., 2024).

That's the trap. Pain leads to drinking. Drinking leads to more pain.

### Nerve damage

About 46% of people with chronic heavy alcohol use show signs of nerve damage (peripheral neuropathy) on nerve testing (Julian et al., 2019). It usually starts in the feet and legs and develops slowly: numbness, tingling, pins and needles. Some people also have burning pain.

### Sleep

Alcohol may knock you out, but it breaks up the second half of your night (Ebrahim et al., 2013). Poor sleep and pain feed each other: bad nights make pain worse, and pain makes nights worse (Finan et al., 2013).

## Mixing alcohol with pain medicine

This is where people get into real trouble. According to the National Institute on Alcohol Abuse and Alcoholism (NIAAA, n.d.), combining alcohol with:

- **Acetaminophen (Tylenol)** can cause liver damage.
- **Ibuprofen or naproxen (Advil, Motrin, Aleve)** can cause stomach upset, bleeding, and ulcers.
- **Opioid painkillers (like Percocet or Vicodin)** raises the risk of overdose and slowed breathing.

If you take any of these, talk to your doctor or pharmacist about alcohol.

## If you want to cut back

**Talk to your doctor before you stop heavy daily drinking.** Alcohol withdrawal can be dangerous. Seizures can start 6 to 48 hours after the last drink, and delirium tremens, the most severe form of withdrawal, can be life-threatening (Harvard Health Publishing, n.d.). A doctor can help you taper safely.

If you want help, SAMHSA's National Helpline is free, confidential, and open 24/7: **1-800-662-4357**.

## Try this

For one week, keep a simple log. Each night, write down three things: how many drinks you had, your pain from 0 to 10, and how your gut felt. Don't change anything yet. Just notice.

Then look at the week and get curious. Not judgmental. Curious.

Notice it. Name it. Choose what's next.
