*Originally answered for an interview with a Florida State University student.*

## Have you seen first-hand the effects of remote learning on students' mental health? If so, how?

I have seen first hand the negative impact of remote learning on students' mental health. Specifically, students with attention difficulties have displayed more difficulty staying on task, completing tasks, finding motivation, and recalling information. Students with social anxiety have also displayed a notable increase in symptoms and desire to avoid social interactions. In depression, lack of motivation usually results in poor academic performance.

## What advice do you have for students struggling with remote learning?

Change it up. Don't just sit at your same desk in your apartment day after day. Spend an afternoon at your favorite coffee shop. Explore libraries all around town and campus. Spend time meeting up with others to engage in social interaction while completing remote learning or other studying to promote socialization and engagement with others. If you are trapped in one space indoors, take frequent breaks outside and stretch regularly.

## How do you think remote learning can affect a student's behavior in a classroom setting?

The more we practice a behavior the better we become at it. If students are not practicing sitting in a classroom as often, it is reasonable to expect some difficulty adjusting their behavior back when in the classroom.

## What are some ways professors can make remote learning easier for students?

If possible, hybrid models of some in-person and some remote learning would be preferred. Professors should also encourage and possibly provide credit for social interactions and social engagements. Professors can also be understanding of the differences in students' abilities to learn from and engage with remote learning. While some students adjust extremely well, others are suffering and struggle much more than they will ever admit.

## Do you believe schools should still utilize video-conferencing programs such as Zoom and Skype?

I think video conferencing programs can be extremely useful in schools. Because of the now widely accepted use of video conferencing, students can be educated by individuals far beyond their college campus. Guest speakers, guest lecturers, and even international or out of state students could be sharing information with the accessibility provided by video conferencing. Video conferencing also allows teachers and professors additional freedom to travel for educational or leisure activities while still accomplishing the teaching goals.
