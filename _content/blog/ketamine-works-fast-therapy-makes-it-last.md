## What ketamine is

Ketamine was developed as an anesthetic. At much lower doses, given by slow IV infusion, it's used for treatment-resistant depression: depression that hasn't improved after trying multiple medications.

IV ketamine for depression is prescribed off-label. A related nasal spray, esketamine (Spravato), was approved by the FDA for treatment-resistant depression in 2019 (American Psychiatric Association, 2019).

## How it's different from typical antidepressants

Common antidepressants like SSRIs work on serotonin and usually take weeks to help. Ketamine works on a different system: glutamate, the brain's main "go" signal.

In plain English: ketamine seems to boost communication between brain cells and help the brain grow new connections, a process called neuroplasticity (Abdallah et al., 2015). Scientists are still working out exactly how. Some of the effect may come from what the body breaks ketamine down into, not ketamine itself (Zanos et al., 2016).

## What the research says

**It can work within a day.** In the first controlled trial in treatment-resistant depression, improvement showed up within 110 minutes of a single infusion, and 71% of patients met response criteria the next day (Zarate et al., 2006). A larger two-site trial found a 64% response rate at 24 hours, compared with 28% for an active placebo (Murrough et al., 2013). The standard dose in these studies is 0.5 mg/kg given over 40 minutes.

**One dose doesn't last.** In that same trial, the difference between ketamine and placebo was gone by day 7 (Murrough et al., 2013). Expert reviews put the benefit of a single dose at roughly three to seven days (McIntyre et al., 2021).

**A short series works better.** When people received six infusions over two weeks, the response rate roughly doubled compared with a single infusion, and 59% responded (Phillips et al., 2019). Most protocols use about six infusions over two to three weeks, and experts recommend checking how well it's working after four to six (McIntyre et al., 2021).

**It held up against ECT.** In a trial of 403 people with treatment-resistant depression, 55% of those given ketamine twice a week for three weeks responded, compared with 41% given electroconvulsive therapy (Anand et al., 2023).

**Remission happens for some.** In an open-label study at four sites, about 52% of 75 people reached remission after three infusions over 11 days (Parikh et al., 2024). There was no comparison group, so read that number as encouraging, not proven.

## Ketamine and suicidal thoughts

This is one of the most important findings, so I want to be precise about it.

Pooling individual data from 10 trials, a single ketamine infusion reduced suicidal thoughts faster than control treatments, starting on day one and lasting up to a week (Wilkinson et al., 2018). In a trial of 80 depressed adults with suicidal thoughts, 55% given ketamine had their suicidal thoughts cut at least in half within 24 hours, compared with 30% given an active placebo (Grunebaum et al., 2018).

In a review of 424 patients at real-world infusion clinics, about half of those who started with suicidal thoughts no longer reported them after six infusions (Oliver et al., 2022). That was a chart review without a control group, so it can't tell us how much was ketamine itself.

If you're having thoughts of suicide right now, call or text 988 in the U.S. You don't have to wait for an infusion to get help.

## Beyond depression

Ketamine is also being studied for trauma. In a small trial of 41 adults with chronic PTSD, a single infusion reduced PTSD symptoms within 24 hours more than an active placebo did (Feder et al., 2014). That's early evidence, not a standard treatment.

## What to expect at an infusion

- A slow IV drip in a medical clinic, usually about 40 minutes.
- You may feel disconnected, calm, emotional, or deeply reflective. That dissociative feeling usually peaks during the infusion and fades within one to two hours.
- Blood pressure often rises during the infusion and usually comes back down within a few hours, so you'll be monitored (McIntyre et al., 2021).
- Plan for a ride home. Your clinic will tell you their rules.

Long-term safety with repeated use is still being studied (McIntyre et al., 2021). Ask your medical team about your own risks.

## Why add therapy?

Here's what I believe, and why I do this work: ketamine can open a window where change feels possible. Without intentional support, your brain can fall back into old patterns. Therapy helps lock in the progress.

Here's what the research can say so far. In a small randomized trial, people who responded to six ketamine infusions and then did 14 weeks of CBT kept more of their improvement on a self-report measure than people who got usual care (Wilkinson et al., 2021). Reviews call ketamine plus therapy promising (Kew et al., 2023). They also say the evidence is early: few studies have tested therapy against no therapy, and the results so far don't show a clear, consistent added benefit (Veraart et al., 2026).

So I won't promise you results. What I can promise is that we'll use the time on purpose:

- Reflect on what comes up during infusions
- Work through emotions or trauma that surface
- Practice skills and new ways of thinking while change feels possible
- Build coping strategies that outlast the medication

## Try this before your next infusion

Write down one thing you want to be different in your life three months from now. Not a symptom. A thing you want to do, feel, or stop doing. Bring it to your next session.

Ketamine can offer fast relief. Therapy helps you build on that relief and turn it into something that lasts.
