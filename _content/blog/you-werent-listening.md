We've all been there. Twenty minutes of bickering and things have only gotten more intense. Voices are louder. Understanding is lower. You don't feel heard, and you are way past the point of listening.

Definitely no constructive communication happening at this point.

Maybe you've both promised to do better before. You meant it. Then emotions took over and the old pattern won, because old patterns are hard to break when your nervous system is in charge.

So here's what to do differently, right in the middle of the argument. It starts with listening.

## 1. Take a break

To truly listen, you may need to calm down first. If things are heated, take a break before you go any further. Even two minutes to breathe and gather your thoughts makes the conversation more focused when you come back to it.

Agree on the break, though. You are not going to calm down if your partner is following you down the hallway asking for your attention.

## 2. Listen closely

One mistake couples make: believing they "have listened" when all they did was let the other person talk.

That isn't listening. It's waiting. You can't listen while you're building your counter argument. You aren't listening if you feel like you've "heard it all before." Listening takes full attention to the present moment.

Make eye contact. Turn off the TV. Put the phone down. For a period of time, give 100% of your attention to the person you love.

## 3. Follow up

When your partner finishes, ask clarifying questions. They help you understand what was actually said, and they help your partner trust that you were listening.

Try: "So what you're saying is..." or "It sounds like..."

Then finish the sentence with what they said, not what you assumed. Summarize. Don't interpret.

## 4. Respond

Calm down. Listen closely. Clarify. Only then do you respond.

When you do, use a softer tone than your partner used. Stick to the one problem in front of you instead of unloading every frustration you've saved up. The goal of conflict in a relationship is to address negative feelings, not create more.

## Try this tonight

Next disagreement, say nothing until you can repeat your partner's point back to them well enough that they say, "Yes, that's it."

It may feel nearly impossible when tensions are high. Becoming a better listener is still the first step toward better communication in your relationship.

Breathe. Listen. Then speak.
