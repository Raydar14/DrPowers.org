"Always trust everyone to be themselves. But trust in the fact you can see them well."

That's DMX, on the *Drink Champs* podcast with N.O.R.E. and DJ EFN ([Moguldom, 2021](https://moguldom.com/347459/dmx-passes-away-at-50-7-positive-quotes-to-remember-the-hip-hop-legend/); [DMX on X](https://x.com/DMX/status/2032585761355149680)). It's one of the smartest things I've heard about trust, and it didn't come from a textbook.

## What he meant

He went on: "Trust a snake to bite you. Trust a liar to lie to you. Trust a thief to steal from you." And then the part that matters most: "Trust them to be them. But know them when you see them."

This isn't cynicism. It's accuracy. A snake that bites hasn't betrayed you. It's being a snake. If you know a thief steals, it isn't a surprise when they do.

The shift is from "How could they do this to me?" to "This is who they are. Now what do I do?"

It reminds me of the old fable of the scorpion and the frog. The scorpion stings the frog carrying it across the river, even though they'll both drown. Why? Because it's a scorpion. Nature wins, even when it's self-destructive.

## Suspicion is expensive

DMX put it simply: "It takes too much energy not to trust someone." His answer was to see people clearly and "stay two steps ahead of them."

Constant doubt is exhausting. Scanning every text, replaying every conversation, waiting for the other shoe to drop. Your nervous system can't rest when it's always on guard.

Trusting someone to be themselves frees up that energy. You stop fighting reality and start working with it.

## Discernment, not hypervigilance

Here's where I want to be careful, as a psychologist.

Hypervigilance is scanning everyone for danger, all the time, whether or not they've given you a reason. It's often what's left behind after we've been hurt. It feels like protection. It mostly feels like fear.

Discernment is different. It's watching what people actually do, believing it, and responding to it. Calm, clear, and specific to the person in front of you.

Not suspicion of everyone. Clarity about someone.

## Acceptance is a boundary

Accepting someone's nature doesn't mean accepting their behavior. It means you stop waiting for them to become someone else.

When a person shows you, again and again, that they lie, you have choices. You can keep hoping, keep arguing, keep getting hurt. Or you can believe them and decide what you'll do: what you share, what you lend, how close you let them stand.

That's a boundary. It's built on who they are, not on who you wish they were.

## Try this

Think of one person who keeps surprising you in the same painful way. Write down what they've actually shown you, not what you hope. Then write one thing you'll do differently, based on that.

Know them. Believe them. Protect your peace.

## With gratitude

With deep gratitude and respect: DMX was an amazing person and artist. A great philosopher of our time. Beyond his time, and lost too soon.
