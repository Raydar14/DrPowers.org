It's 11:40 PM. You're exhausted. You're also on your third "one more video."

Your brain isn't broken. It's overstimulated and confused about what time it is. The fix isn't willpower. It's rhythm.

This is my protocol for resetting sleep, restoring mental energy, and sleeping like a monk.

## 1. Keep the same sleep schedule every day

Your circadian rhythm runs on light, dark, and routine. When your schedule bounces around, your brain gets mixed signals about when to be awake and when to rest.

- Go to bed and wake up within the same 30-minute window every day.
- Skip the big weekend sleep-ins. Sleeping late on weekends creates "social jet lag," and your brain pays for it on Monday.

Regular sleep supports deep, slow-wave sleep and a steadier, clearer prefrontal cortex: the part of your brain that plans, focuses, and keeps you from texting your ex.

## 2. Shut off screens 90 minutes before bed

Blue light suppresses melatonin, your body's sleep signal. Fast, scrolling content keeps your reward system firing and your brain wired.

- Screens off at least 90 minutes before bed.
- If you can't, use night mode, dim the screen, or wear blue-light glasses.
- Swap in a book, stretching, journaling, rain sounds, or a sleep podcast.

## 3. Your bed is for sleep (and intimacy)

Your brain builds associations. If your bed is where you work, scroll, and worry, your brain stops linking it with rest.

- Only lie down when you're sleepy.
- Awake after 20 minutes? Get up, do something calm, and come back when you're drowsy.

## 4. Make your room cool, dark, and quiet

- **Temperature:** 60 to 67°F (15 to 19°C)
- **Light:** Blackout curtains or a sleep mask.
- **Sound:** Earplugs, or white or pink noise.
- **Scent:** Lavender or vetiver, if you like them.

## 5. Watch the stimulants and the sedatives

- **Caffeine:** None after 2 PM. It blocks adenosine, the chemical that builds sleep pressure, and delays deep sleep.
- **Alcohol:** It may knock you out, but it disrupts REM sleep and breaks up the second half of your night.
- **Nicotine:** A stimulant. Same problem as caffeine.

## 6. Move your body (but not right before bed)

Exercise lowers stress and strengthens your sleep rhythm.

- Move in the morning or afternoon.
- Skip intense workouts in the 2 to 3 hours before bed.

## 7. Build a 90-minute wind-down

You need time to shift from "on" to "off." Nobody goes from inbox to deep sleep in four minutes.

- A warm bath or shower about 90 minutes before bed. It sounds backward, but warming up helps your core temperature drop afterward, and that drop is your body's cue to sleep.
- Light stretching or gentle yoga.
- A journal brain dump to get the thoughts out of your head and onto paper.
- Herbal tea, if it agrees with you.
- A physical book or slow music.

## 8. Track it

What gets noticed gets changed.

- A sleep app or wearable (Oura, Whoop, Sleep Cycle), or
- A simple journal: bedtime, wake time, caffeine, mood.

If you like numbers: **Sleep score = (time asleep minus time restless) ÷ time in bed.**

Many people notice better mood, more energy, and fewer screen cravings within a week or two of steady practice.

## The loop

Less screen time leads to better sleep. Better sleep leads to a steadier reward system. A steadier reward system means fewer cravings for the screen. Feed the loop daily.

## My before-bed routine

Here's what it looks like with a 10:30 PM bedtime. Shift the times to fit your night.

### 9:00 PM: Power down

- All screens off.
- Lights low and warm: amber bulbs, lamps, candles.
- Warm bath or shower now, so your body has time to cool afterward. Add Epsom salts if you like.

### 9:15 PM: Set the room

- Drop the temperature to 60 to 67°F.
- Lavender or chamomile, if you use oils.

### 9:30 PM: Move and breathe

- Gentle stretches: child's pose, cat-cow, yin yoga.
- Breathe in for 4, hold for 7, out for 8. Repeat a few rounds. This shifts your body toward rest-and-digest.

### 9:45 PM: Wind down your mind

- A physical, not-too-exciting book.
- Gratitude journaling or a brain dump.
- Mindful doodling.

### 10:30 PM: In bed with intention

- Room cool, dark, and quiet.
- Phone outside the room or on airplane mode.
- Lights off or red.
- Let go of the day. Don't chase sleep. Let it come to you.

## Why it works

| What you do | What it does |
|---|---|
| Screens off | Lets melatonin rise and calms your reward system |
| Slow breathing | Activates the vagus nerve and lowers anxiety |
| Journaling | Moves rumination out of your head |
| Warm bath, then cool room | Triggers the temperature drop that starts sleep |
| Low light | Supports natural melatonin |

## Start small

Don't try to do all of this tonight. Pick one thing. Layer slowly.

The biggest win isn't the perfect routine. It's doing the routine consistently.

Same time. Same rhythm. Sweet dreams.
