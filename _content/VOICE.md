# The voice of Dr. Raychel Powers

Use this guide for every blog post, page, caption, and bio, on every site, whoever or whatever is writing. If a piece doesn't sound like this, it isn't finished.

## Who's talking

A psychologist who has sat in courtrooms, therapy rooms, prisons, and protests, and talks like a real person. Smart without lecturing. Warm without being soft. Honest even when it's uncomfortable.

Her ethos, in her words: **clarity over judgment, curiosity over shame, and creativity that heals.**

## Five traits

1. **Direct.** Say the thing in the first line. No warm-up, no throat clearing.
2. **Warm, never precious.** Normalize the struggle before fixing it. "We've all been there." "We are all human. We are inherently flawed."
3. **The brain, in plain English.** Science explained like you'd explain it to a smart friend. "The brain does not un-learn very well."
4. **Bold, a little irreverent.** Take a stand. Allow one sharp edge per piece. "Every damn space." "Don't hate me cus u ain't me."
5. **Self-kindness is the through-line.** Most pieces come back, somehow, to treating yourself the way you'd treat someone you love.

## Signature moves (protect these)

- **Threes.** Lists, rhythms, and closers in threes. "Justice that frees. Therapy that empowers. Creativity that heals." "Pause. Breathe. Be." "Three minutes, three times a day, for three weeks."
- **Contrast pairs.** "Ketamine works fast. Therapy makes it last." "With the system, around it, and against it when it needed to be." "Clarity over judgment."
- **Alliteration with a point.** "Courtrooms, bedrooms, and boardrooms." "From kink to court."
- **Short stacked lines for personal truth.** "Not rich, not poor. Not popular or smart." Use for openings, closings, and anything about her own life.
- **Sensory metaphors, especially food.** "Find it and savor it like the last bite, of the last piece, of the world's best dessert."
- **Play.** Turn practices into games, missions, and rounds (Metta in the Wild).
- **Memorable self-statements.** "I have me all to myself. If you had me all to yourself, there wouldn't be enough of me left for me."
- **One moment of emphasis.** One word in CAPS or one curse word per piece, at most, where it truly matters.
- **Real life, when it earns its place.** Personal history (a cornfield in Indiana, federal death row, Long COVID) appears when it makes the point land, never as decoration. Never invent stories or details she hasn't shared.

## Who "I," "you," and "we" are

- **I** for her views, her stance, and her experience.
- **You** when speaking to the reader. Most of the writing.
- **We** for the shared human condition ("We are all human") or for the actual team at a group practice (Powers of Mind). Never a corporate "we" for her solo work.

## Shape of a blog post

- **Title:** sentence case, plain-spoken, a claim or a command, often two beats. "You weren't listening. You were waiting to talk." "Sleep like a monk." No colons stacked with subtitles, no "Understanding X: A Journey of Y."
- **Opening:** a moment the reader recognizes, or a blunt truth. Never a definition, never "This article explores," never "In today's world."
- **Body:** short H2s in plain words. Paragraphs of one to four sentences. Lists for steps only.
- **Science:** say the finding, say what it means for the reader's life, cite (Author, year) in the text and in full at the end. If a claim can't be sourced, cut it or label it as her clinical view.
- **Close:** a small "Try this" (one thing, doable today) and a final line that lands, usually three beats. No "In conclusion," no summary of what was just said.

## Sentences

- Contractions, always.
- Mix short punches with medium sentences. A one-line paragraph is allowed when it earns it.
- Plain verbs: use, help, try, notice, stop, start.
- Keep her original sentences when they're strong. Fix grammar, never flatten her.

## Never

- Em dashes (—). Rewrite with a comma, period, colon, or parentheses.
- Eyebrow labels (small uppercase labels above headings).
- Icons in graphics unless she asks for them. Emoji in long-form writing. Hashtags in blog posts.
- AI and corporate filler: "In today's fast-paced world," "This article explores," "delve," "navigate," "journey" (as filler), "unlock," "empower your," "holistic approach," "profound impact," "it's important to note," "in conclusion," "game-changer," "dive deep," "transformative," "embark," "we are dedicated to," "unique ways to cope with modern-day problems."
- Stacked hedges ("may potentially help some people").
- Diagnosing the reader, or promising outcomes ("benefits in 5 days").

## Names and credentials

- First mention in a bio: **Dr. Raychel Powers, Psy.D.** After that: **Dr. Powers**.
- Blog byline: Dr. Raychel Powers, Psy.D.
- "Dr. Raychel" is fine for warm, personal-brand touches (Dr. Raychel recommends). "Raya" and "Ray" belong only to personal brands (Raya Sunshine, Ray Playz).
- Brand names keep their own styling: JoURney wOrKz, BE333, Powers of Mind, Powers of Forensic Minds.

## Clinical guardrails

- Every post ends with the site's clinical disclaimer (the blog adds it automatically).
- Service descriptions say where she is licensed (Florida, Colorado, telehealth).
- Psychedelic and sexual-health topics: education first, sources cited, no promises.

## Quick test before anything goes live

1. Does the first line say something real?
2. Is there at least one line only she would write?
3. Did a "try this" and a closing line make it in?
4. Any em dashes, eyebrows, or banned words?
5. Is every factual claim sourced or clearly her opinion?
