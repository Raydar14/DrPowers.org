Power of Sleep Protocol

Dr. Raychel Powers, Psy.D. October 17, 2026 Mind & Body 4 min read

For resetting dopamine, restoring mental energy, and sleeping like a monk.

1. Stick to a consistent sleep-wake schedule

Why it matters: Your circadian rhythm is regulated by light/dark cues and routine. Disruption confuses sleep-wake signals and affects dopamine receptor sensitivity.

What to do:

  • Go to bed and wake up within the same 30-minute window every day.
  • Avoid drastic sleep-ins on weekends (“social jetlag” reduces dopamine responsiveness).

Science: Regular sleep increases slow-wave activity and stabilizes the prefrontal cortex.

2. Disconnect from screens 1 to 2 hours before bed

Why it matters: Blue light inhibits melatonin production. Fast content overstimulates dopamine, keeping your brain wired.

What to do:

  • Shut off screens at least 90 minutes before bed.
  • Use blue-light glasses, dim your screen, or switch to night mode if needed.
  • Replace screens with books, stretching, journaling, or audio like rain sounds or sleep podcasts.

Bonus: Avoiding screen-time spikes allows dopamine receptors to recover faster.

3. Reserve your bed only for sleep (and intimacy)

Why it matters: Your brain builds associations. If your bed is for work or screens, your brain won’t associate it with rest.

Fix it:

  • Only lie down when sleepy.
  • If awake after 20 minutes, get up, do a calm task, then return when drowsy.

4. Optimize the sleep environment (cool, dark, quiet)

Why it matters: Sensory input like light or heat affects brain readiness for rest.

Make it ideal:

  • Temperature: 60 to 67°F (15 to 19°C)
  • Light: Use blackout curtains or a sleep mask.
  • Sound: Use earplugs or white/pink noise.
  • Scent tip: Lavender and vetiver oils can promote calm.

5. Avoid stimulants and sedatives (caffeine, nicotine, alcohol)

Caffeine: Avoid after 2 PM. Blocks adenosine and delays deep sleep.

Alcohol: Disrupts REM and causes fragmented sleep.

Why it matters for dopamine: These substances dysregulate dopamine and hinder recovery.

6. Move your body (but not right before bed)

Why it matters: Exercise improves dopamine, lowers stress, and supports sleep rhythms.

How to time it:

  • Exercise in the morning or afternoon.
  • Avoid intense workouts 2 to 3 hours before bed.

7. Create a calming wind-down ritual (90-minute rule)

Why it matters: You need time to shift from “on” to “off.”

Build your ritual:

  • 90 minutes before: light stretching, yoga, or a warm bath.
  • Journal: a brain dump reduces overthinking.
  • Herbal tea (if tolerated).
  • Read a physical book or listen to slow music.

8. Track and experiment

Why it matters: Personalized awareness improves results.

Tools:

  • Sleep apps (Oura, Whoop, Sleep Cycle)
  • Manual journaling: bedtime, wake time, caffeine, mood

Sleep Score = (Time asleep minus time restless) / Time in bed

Benefits appear in just 5 to 7 days: better mood, energy, less screen craving.

The dopamine-sleep connection

  • Deep sleep improves dopamine receptor sensitivity.
  • Less screen time → better sleep → more dopamine → less craving.
  • It’s a loop. Feed it daily.

The ultimate before-bed routine

Designed to reset dopamine, improve sleep quality, and support long-term screen detox.

90 minutes before bed (e.g., 9:00 PM if sleeping at 10:30 PM)

1. Power down all screens

  • Blue light delays melatonin.
  • Fast digital content floods dopamine.
  • Replace with amber lights, candles, or dim warm lighting.

2. Set the sleep environment

  • Drop room temperature to 60 to 67°F.
  • Use calming essential oils like lavender or chamomile.

60 minutes before bed

3. Gentle movement and breath

  • Yin yoga, child’s pose, or cat-cow stretches
  • Breathing technique: inhale 4, hold 7, exhale 8
  • Helps activate rest-and-digest mode

45 minutes before bed

4. Mental wind-down

  • Physical book (non-stimulating)
  • Gratitude journaling
  • Mindful doodling
  • Optional: brain dump to clear your mind

15 minutes before bed

5. Warm shower or bath

  • Helps cool core body temperature afterward, which promotes sleep onset
  • Optional: add Epsom salts for magnesium

Bedtime (target: 10:30 PM)

6. In bed with intention

  • Room: cool, dark, and quiet
  • Phone: outside the room or on airplane mode
  • Lights: off or red spectrum
  • Let go of the day. Don’t chase sleep.

Why this works

Element Brain/body impact
Screen removal Lowers dopamine and increases melatonin
Breathwork Activates vagus nerve, reduces anxiety
Journaling Offloads rumination, improves focus
Warm bath Triggers drop in body temperature needed for sleep onset
Low light Stimulates natural melatonin production

Bonus tips

  • Track sleep and mood for self-awareness.
  • Don’t rush the process. Layer slowly.
  • The biggest win is starting the routine consistently.
